Educational infographic, clean flat design, muted parchment background with classical antiquity color palette (terracotta, olive, gold, ink blue), 16:9 landscape aspect ratio.

Title at top center: "Why Owl & Sheep Mean Money" with small Korean subtitle "돈의 상징: 올빼미와 양".

Layout: two large side-by-side comparison panels under the title, and one wide bottom strip.

LEFT PANEL — header "GREECE: The Owl (올빼미)":
- An ancient Athenian silver coin showing an owl, drawn flat-style, label "Athens emblem on coins".
- Arrow from coin to a money bag, label "Owl = money".
- Below, a 3-step mini comic strip with arrows: (1) man hiding coins under roof tiles, label "Gylippus hides funds"; (2) servant pointing at roof, speech bubble "Owls under the tiles!"; (3) judges nodding over recovered coins, label "Money recovered".

RIGHT PANEL — header "ROME: The Sheep (양)":
- A flat icon of a sheep and an ox side by side, label "Wealth = livestock (pecus)".
- Arrow to a bronze coin stamped with a sheep and ox, label "King Servius' first coin".
- Word evolution shown as arrow chain: "pecus" → "pecunia (money)".
- Small badge: rich man with sheep, label "Rich = pecorosi".

BOTTOM STRIP: centered figure of Ripa's Pecunia personification — woman in yellow, white and dark robe, owl on her head, sheep at her feet, holding minting tools — label "Ripa's PECUNIA", with two thin arrows pointing to her owl from the left panel and to her sheep from the right panel.

All labels short (3-5 words), clear sans-serif lettering, generous spacing, no long sentences.
